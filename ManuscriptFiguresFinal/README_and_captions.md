# Final manuscript figures

Run from RALSA: `julia --startup-file=no --project=. ManuscriptFiguresFinal/build_figures.jl`.

PNG and editable SVG outputs are in outputs/. Existing figures and analyses are preserved. Figure 3 contains the coded panels only; its bottom-right quadrant is intentionally empty. Figure 4 includes schematic retention scenarios, which can be replaced by final artwork. The code does not fit new distribution models.

## Figure 2
(a) Expected species, regional-interaction and local-support retention under uniform fixed-count site removal. Faint curves show individual datasets; thick curves are equal-dataset means, interpolated linearly onto a common removal grid for display. Species retention includes confirmed occurrence-only species and pools guilds with equal species weight. (b) Binned support-frequency distributions as horizontal stacked bars, each summing to 100% of regional links. (c) Mean regional-minus-local retention gap, in percentage points, averaged over exact integer removal levels through 80%, including zero. Dataset rows in b and c run from smaller to larger gap. Panel a uses the same drawing order; line colours denote quantities rather than datasets. Each retention quantity is relative to its own original total. Site count, effort and extent affect comparisons.

## Figure 3 coded panels
(b) Quercus frequency distributions: degree-weighted species occupancy, co-occurrence among known interacting pairs, and realised local support for those same pairs. Each distribution has its own denominator. (c) Standardised third-moment skewness across the three stages for all datasets; Quercus is highlighted. Nine datasets increase at both steps; Salix decreases slightly at the first step. The example illustrates an observed pattern, not a universally monotonic sequence. Species weights use initial regional degree. The upper half contains the frequencies, lower-left the condensed skewness plot, and lower-right is reserved. The landscape drawing is not included.

## Figure 4
(a) Qualitative scenarios for the fraction of initial partners retained versus initial degree: specialist advantage, neutral relation, and generalist advantage. Lines are schematic, with no fitted model or numerical prediction. (b) Empirical mean local support per partner versus initial regional degree, pooling consumers and resources. Each point is one species; orange lines are ordinary least-squares regressions with an intercept, checked against saved coefficients. No binned means are shown. Panel b measures support, not retention directly: mean support alone does not fully specify retention, which depends on all constituent link supports. Pooling guilds can affect slopes; the existing guild-specific analyses remain available.

## Figure 5
(a) Garraf PP2 consumer degree CCDFs at baseline and after nominal 40% and 80% site removal, rounded to attainable site counts. Post-removal curves are arithmetic means of the 100 separately normalised positive-degree CCDFs from the exact same saved removal orders as model selection. Disconnected species are excluded. The x-axis is linear and the probability axis logarithmic. Lines connect changes in cumulative probability and retain their final positive endpoints; no stair rendering or fitted curves. Very low average tail probabilities can represent rare retention across sequences. (b) Percentage of datasets assigned to each best-supported family. Baseline uses AIC weights; removal states use the family with highest mean AIC weight across the same 100 sequences. Unchanged family labels do not imply unchanged parameters or structure; model uncertainty is not shown in these bars.
