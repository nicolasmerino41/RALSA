# Draft figure captions

**Figure 1. Local support and regional interaction persistence.** (A) Illustrative opportunity and interaction matrices for five known regional links across five sites. An opportunity indicates that both partners occur at a site; an interaction can occur only at an opportunity. C is the number of opportunity sites and K the number of sites supporting the interaction. All five links have positive initial support. (B) Removing sites 1 and 2 reduces the number of local interaction occurrences from nine to five and regional link richness from five to four. This is one selected removal outcome, not the expectation under random removal. (C) Exact expected regional retention under uniform removal of m sites without replacement, averaged over these five links, and expected local-support retention, 1 − m/N. A link with K supporting sites survives with probability 1 − binomial(m,K)/binomial(N,K), with extinction probability zero when m < K. Shading shows the regional–local retention gap. Points mark integer removal levels; connecting lines guide the eye. The marked 0–80% range defines the integer levels, including zero, used for arithmetic mean-gap summaries. The example is illustrative throughout.

**Figure 2. Empirical support distributions and retention under random site removal.** (A) Fractions of observed regional links supported by one, two, three to four, five to eight, or at least nine distinct sites. N is the number of recorded sampled sites in each dataset. (B) Equal-dataset mean expected regional-link retention, the descriptive minimum–maximum range across the ten datasets, and expected local-support retention. Exact curves for uniform fixed-count removal were linearly interpolated to a common removal-fraction grid for display only. The range describes differences among datasets, not uncertainty in the mean. (C) Dataset-specific mean regional–local retention gaps, in percentage points. Each mean gives equal weight to that dataset’s integer removal levels satisfying m/N ≤ 0.8, including zero. These summaries use the original integer levels, not the interpolated display curves. Regional interaction identities and the sampled site universe remain fixed.

**Figure 3. Feasible gaps and conditional allocation of local support.** (A) Exact feasible ranges of the mean gap conditional on site count N, regional link count L, and total local support M (thick light-grey segments), and ranges additionally constrained by the observed pair-specific co-occurrence opportunities (thin dark-grey segments). All regional links must retain positive initial support. Green segments show the central 95% of 5,000 saved exact conditional allocation draws, and open circles their means; purple diamonds show empirical mean gaps. The allocation null is uniform over eligible binary pair–site interaction matrices conditional on the opportunity matrix, M, and positive support for every known regional link. It does not fix site-specific interaction totals. Mean gaps are arithmetic averages over integer removal levels m/N ≤ 0.8, including zero, and are expressed in percentage points. (B) Empirical singleton percentage minus the corresponding allocation-null mean. The green interval is the null’s central 95% interval after subtracting that same mean; its open circle therefore lies at zero. Intervals describe variation among conditional allocations, not confidence intervals or variation among removal sequences. The plotted feasible bounds and allocation draws are existing exact-analysis outputs; no new sampler was run.

**Figure 4. Gap prediction under two validation tasks.** Paired dataset-level mean absolute errors for common-probability and persistent-pair models under (A) entire-dataset holdout and (B) within-system holdout. The common-probability model assigns one realisation probability to eligible opportunities; the persistent-pair model allows pair-level differences that persist across sites. Entire-dataset holdout fits on the other nine datasets and uses the target’s known regional identities, opportunity matrix and site count. Within-system holdout retains the existing split with approximately 20% of sites withheld and predicts completion of the partly observed landscape using the training-site interactions and known opportunities. It is a different, less information-restricted task; the two panels use different horizontal scales. Prediction curves average the existing 400 conditional matrix draws per dataset, model and task. Errors compare predicted and empirical expected gaps over integer removal levels m/N ≤ 0.8, including zero, in percentage points. The last row gives the unweighted mean of the ten dataset errors. Models within each task share evaluation levels and cells. Shared sites in the two Gottin datasets, and unresolved overlap among datasets from a shared collection, mean entire-dataset holdout is not independent landscape validation.
