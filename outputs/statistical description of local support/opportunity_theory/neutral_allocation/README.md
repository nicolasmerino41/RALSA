# Random allocation within the opportunity-constrained envelope

Run from RALSA:

```
julia --startup-file=no --project=. scripts/neutral_allocation.jl
```

The script accepts repository and output-directory arguments. It preserves all existing analyses and writes only to `opportunity_theory/neutral_allocation` by default.

## Null hypothesis

Every eligible binary pair–site allocation is equally likely, conditional on the observed opportunities, exactly M total occurrences and at least one occurrence for every known regional link. N, L and every C are fixed; individual K and local site totals are not fixed. This is a specified neutral allocation rule, not the only possible meaning of randomness.

Because the gap depends on row supports rather than their particular locations, we sample K directly with probability proportional to product_l choose(C_l,K_l), subject to 1≤K_l≤C_l and sum K=M. This counts the eligible site arrangements for each support vector. Support vectors themselves are not given equal weights. Uniformly choosing K_l supporting sites for each sampled row would recover a uniform full allocation; those extra site draws cannot change the expected gap and are omitted.

Dynamic programming and backward sampling generate 5,000 independent conditional draws per dataset. A common Bernoulli probability centres the numerical calculation around M; it cancels exactly upon conditioning on total M and is not an ecological fitted parameter. Prefix tables are rescaled to avoid underflow. No MCMC, approximate activity matching, one-forced-occurrence shortcut, or rejection of zero-support links is used. Exact enumeration of 94 small support configurations agrees with the sampler's calculated probabilities to within 1.2×10⁻¹⁶. Every draw is checked for its constraints.

For each draw, the gap is calculated analytically for fixed-count random removal. The primary response is the arithmetic mean over integer removal counts m/N≤0.8, including zero, matching the previous analysis. Z is this mean gap's position between the exact integrated bounds. All draws have exactly the empirical M, so their envelopes are identical to the empirical envelope. The intervals are central 95% null-allocation intervals, not confidence intervals or variation among removal sequences.

Seeds are 731113 plus dataset index. All earlier input hashes are checked, and the reused link-capacity and envelope tables are additionally fingerprinted in `input_versions.csv`. Confirmed occurrence corrections and all parent-analysis limitations remain: interaction-derived occupancy, excluded uncertain corrections, known regional identities, shared Gottin sites and unresolved provider-collection overlap. No new occurrence inference is made.

## Results

**All ten empirical mean gaps fall below their dataset's central 95% random-allocation interval.** Observed gaps are 1.10–5.50 percentage points smaller than the random means. All ten also have higher observed singleton fractions than the random means.

The empirical constrained positions span 0.435–0.621; random mean positions span 0.590–0.841. Thus random allocations do not fill the envelope uniformly and do not generally centre on its midpoint. The empirical midpoint clustering is a consistent departure below the specified neutral expectation, not its reproduction.

Two-sided Monte Carlo tail probabilities range from approximately 0.00040 (the simulation resolution) to 0.00560. These are individual conditional comparisons; the ten datasets are not treated as independent replicates for a combined significance test.

This supports unequal realisation beyond that generated by opportunities and fixed total activity alone: more links are singletons, while the remaining activity is carried by other links. It does not identify whether persistent compatibility, detection, abundance, sampling or another process causes that departure. Nor does it show that empirical systems universally occupy the midpoint.

`neutral_comparison.png` shows null intervals and empirical values on both Z and percentage-point scales. `summary.csv` contains the numerical comparisons and singleton fractions; `draws.csv` contains every draw; `sampler_check.csv` records exact verification.
