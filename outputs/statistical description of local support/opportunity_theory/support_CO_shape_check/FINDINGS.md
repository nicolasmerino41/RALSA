# Findings

Support has a higher coefficient of variation (SD/mean) than co-occurrence in all ten datasets. Exploratory paired-bootstrap intervals for the difference exclude zero in eight (all except Nahuel and Garraf HP). These intervals assume independent pairs despite shared species/sites, so this is not a definitive significance claim.

The largest relative CV difference is Gottin HP (1.375 vs 0.759; approximately 81% higher). Gottin PP is approximately 42% higher. Garraf PP2 is only approximately 8% higher, consistent with its visually close curves; Garraf HP and Nahuel have little separation by CV.

Gini differences are not uniform: K has lower Gini in Nahuel, Gottin PP, Garraf HP, Garraf PP and Montseny. Hence higher relative dispersion is not equivalent to uniformly greater inequality or uniformly heavier tails. CV weights extreme values differently from Gini.

Conclusion: local support is not simply a uniformly scaled copy of the opportunity distribution across all datasets. There are recurring relative-dispersion differences, but no single direction of change across all shape summaries. This comparison does not identify different named distribution families and does not establish a mechanism; discrete sampling/thinning itself can alter shape.
