# Statistical description of local support

**The datasets share a broad, strongly right-skewed shape, but these descriptions do not establish one common statistical distribution.** In all ten datasets, K=1 is the mode and the mean exceeds the median. Single-site links comprise 39.4% (Garraf PP2) to 70.8% (Garraf HP). Means range from 1.79 to 4.43 supporting sites; medians are 1–2. Most regional links therefore have little spatial replication, alongside a smaller set with repeated support.

## Differences and concentration

Garraf PP2 has the fewest singletons and the highest mean and 90th percentile (12 sites). Garraf HP has the most singletons and the lowest mean. Garraf PP combines a low median (1) with a long, sparse tail reaching 38 of 40 sites; it has the largest coefficient of variation (1.68). Thus differences involve both the common low-support links and the sparsely populated tail. Individual frequency sequences need not decrease at every K.

The top 10% of links account for **28.2–45.5% of total support**, compared with 10% under equal support. The largest top-decile shares occur in Gottin HP (45.5%) and Garraf PP (44.7%); the smallest is Nahuel (28.2%). Gini coefficients range from 0.36 to 0.53. Concentration curves cross, so neither the singleton fraction nor the top-decile share supplies a complete ordering of distributional inequality. These curves concern concentration across interaction identities, not across sites.

## Raw versus relative support

The raw-K curves share the same qualitative form but do not coincide, and scaling by N does not produce an apparent universal curve. Salix–Galpar and Gottin HP have almost identical mean K (3.306), yet mean K/N is 0.0097 versus 0.1033 because N is 341 versus 32. Nahuel has only eight sites and the largest mean K/N (0.256), despite mean K being only 2.05. These are descriptive contrasts, not evidence that site count alone causes the differences.

N ranges from 8 to 341. Sampling extent, site grain, effort, detection and the underlying ecological distribution can all influence observed K and which regional interactions enter the K>0 sample. K/N changes the scale and its minimum possible value is 1/N; it does not standardise those processes or recover undetected links. No formal shared-family test or sampling correction was performed.

## Consequences for random site removal

Single-site links disappear when their one site is removed, while repeatedly supported links persist until all supporting sites are lost. For m sites removed uniformly without replacement, survival is 1-C(m,K)/C(N,K), with extinction probability zero when m<K. Averaging over the support distribution determines expected regional retention; expected proportional local-support retention is 1-m/N. Thus the full K distribution, together with N, describes their expected separation. Concentration alone is not a sufficient robustness metric, and no site-priority or spatial-overlap conclusion follows from these curves.

The shared qualitative shape motivates, at most, future comparisons of a geometric distribution truncated at N as a simple decreasing baseline and a zero-truncated beta-binomial as a more flexible bounded family. Neither is fitted here. These plots do not establish a power law, a common family or a mechanism.

## Input limitation

This uses the current validated interaction snapshot, retaining its documented unresolved one-link discrepancies for Garraf HP and Olot. Confirmed occurrence restorations exist in the separate zero-presence sensitivity analysis but have not replaced the main occupancy exports. Those restorations change co-occurrence-based quantities; they do not change these K distributions while interaction records and the site universe remain fixed. Complete independently sampled occupancy and a census of any wholly unrecorded sampled sites are not established by these exports.
